This page follows the *MMRC26 Official Rulebook & Competition Guide*, revision 1.1. If this page
and the printed rulebook disagree, the printed rulebook wins, and the judges have the final say
on what it means.

## The objective

Build an autonomous mouse that drives itself from a corner of the maze to the four cells at its
centre. Speed matters, but so does finishing. Your score divides your successful runs by your
fastest time, so a mouse that finishes often can beat a mouse that is only fast.

## Eligibility

MMRC 26 is open to currently enrolled university students. You can enter alone or as a team of
up to three members. Each team may enter one micromouse.

If there is time on the day, each team gives a short talk about its mouse before the contest.
The talk lasts five minutes at most.

## The maze

The competition maze is a 10×10 grid of 18cm cells with 12mm-thick walls, built to IEEE
micromouse standard dimensions. Measured between the inside faces of the walls, one cell is 18cm
square, with a 5% tolerance. The corners of each cell meet at a 1.2cm lattice post, and at least
one wall is attached to every post.

The start cell is in one of the four corners and has walls on three sides. The start line sits
between that cell and the next one clockwise, so your clock starts when the mouse leaves the
corner, not when you let go of it. The goal is the block of four cells at the centre, and it has
one entrance.

**The centre is an island.** Its walls don't touch the rest of the maze, so a mouse that follows
one wall the whole way will never get there. It will go round the outer wall until time runs out.
There are several routes to the centre, and wall-hugging finds none of them.

Don't trust your sensors to see perfect colours. The wall sides are white, the tops are red and
the floor is black, but the maze is painted plywood and the paint fades in places. The floor can
be slippery. Lighting, temperature and humidity depend on the venue: expect sunlight, fluorescent
or incandescent light, or a mix of all three.

The competition layout stays secret until the day. The maze below is a new random one each time
you load the page. Press **New maze** to see another.

## The robot

- **Self-contained.** Onboard battery and onboard logic. No remote control, no external
  processing and no wireless data link.
- **No combustion.** The mouse can't run on anything that burns.
- **A footprint no larger than 25cm × 25cm.** Length and width must stay within 25cm for the
  whole match. If your mouse unfolds or extends, the unfolded shape has to fit as well. There is
  no height limit.
- **Nothing left behind.** No part of the mouse may come off in the maze.
- **Hands off the walls.** The mouse can't jump over, fly over or climb the walls, and it can't
  scratch, cut, burn, mark or damage them.

Breaking any of these rules means immediate disqualification.

## The match

Each team gets **8 minutes** at the maze, starting when the contest administrator gives you
access. The clock keeps running between runs. Adjusting the mouse, changing a battery or
restarting all use up the same 8 minutes as your runs.

You can make as many runs as fit in that time, and each one starts from the start cell. Every run
that reaches the centre gets a run time, and your fastest one is your **official time**.

- You can abort a run at any point.
- If you touch the mouse during a run, that run is aborted and you must take the mouse off the
  maze.
- After the mouse crosses the finish line you can pick it up whenever you like. The run time is
  already recorded.

After the maze is revealed, you can't give the mouse any information about it. You can change
switch positions, but you can't load a map. The judges collect and check every team's source code.

## Search runs and speed runs

The rules don't make you explore before you race, but your mouse can only plan a route through
cells it has already mapped. Most teams use the first run to find the walls and the later runs to
go fast.

A careful search costs time you won't get back. A rushed one leaves gaps in the map your speed run
has to guess through.

## Scoring

Both phases use the same formula:

> **Final score = (total successful runs ÷ official time) × 1000**

The higher score ranks higher. The rulebook gives an example. A team with four runs and a best
time of 25 seconds scores 160.0. A team with one run and a best time of 18 seconds scores 55.6.
The second mouse is faster, but the first team ranks higher.

(The printed rulebook shows the second score as 55.5. The exact value is 1000 ÷ 18 = 55.55…, so
this site rounds it to 55.6. The ranking is the same either way.)

**If a mouse doesn't reach the centre.** Any mouse that reaches the centre within its 8 minutes
ranks above every mouse that doesn't. Mice that never reach it are ranked by how far along the
shortest traversable path they got, and the lowest remaining distance ranks highest.

## The tournament

**Check-in closes at 09:00.** The organisers' software then draws a random running order and gives
each team a judging slot. If you miss your slot, you lose your Phase 1 run and drop out of the
competition.

**Phase 1: the qualifiers.** Every registered team runs on the same standard maze, one team at a
time. Each team gets the full 8 minutes and is scored with the formula above.

**Phase 2: the knockout league.** The teams that go through are seeded into a single-elimination
bracket. Two teams run at the same time on identical mazes placed side by side, sharing one
8-minute clock. The team with the higher score in each pair goes through.

## Awards

- **1st place, MMRC26 Champion:** winner of the Grand Final.
- **2nd place:** runner-up in the Grand Final.
- **3rd place:** winner of the semi-final runners-up bracket.
- **Overall Highest Score:** the best single score in any Phase 2 match.
- **Best Code:** the best logic, algorithmic efficiency or mapping technique, judged during the
  source code review.
- **Best Creative Design:** the best physical build, such as hardware solutions, custom PCB work or
  chassis design.

## Conduct

The judges may disqualify a team for unsportsmanlike conduct, tampering with the maze or breaking
the rules. The judges' decisions are final.

## Terminology

Words the judges use, and what they mean.

- **Micromouse:** the autonomous robot your team built.
- **Cell:** one 18cm square of the maze.
- **Lattice point (post):** the 1.2cm square where four cells meet and the walls connect.
- **Start square:** the corner cell, with walls on three sides, where every run begins.
- **Destination zone:** the four cells at the centre, with one entrance.
- **Start line / finish line:** the invisible lines that start and stop the clock.
- **Match:** your 8-minute slot at the maze.
- **Run:** one attempt from the start square to the centre.
- **Official time:** your fastest run time in the match.
- **Touch:** any contact with the mouse after a run has started. It ends the run.
- **Abort:** ending a run on purpose, usually to rescue a stuck mouse.
- **Island goal:** a centre whose walls don't connect to the rest of the maze. The MMRC 26 maze
  has one.
- **Wall-hugging:** following one wall the whole way. With an island goal, the mouse goes round
  forever.
- **Search run:** a run that explores the maze and maps the walls.
- **Speed run:** a later run that uses that map to go fast.
- **Shortest traversable path:** the shortest route to the centre that avoids the walls. It is
  used to rank mice that never reach the centre.
- **Simply connected maze:** a maze with no loops and no walls standing on their own.
