MMRC 26 should be a competition people are glad they entered. This code applies
to everyone at the event and on this site: competitors, mentors, volunteers,
judges, organizers and spectators.

## What is expected

The following is expected of everyone named above.

Treat other people as people. Be patient with teams who are newer at this than
you are. Assume the person you are talking to is acting in good faith until they
show you otherwise.

Respect the venue, the equipment, and the maze. Follow instructions from
organizers and volunteers, particularly around safety.

## What is not acceptable

The following are treated as breaches of this code:

- Harassment of any kind, including comments about gender, gender identity,
  sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race,
  ethnicity, nationality, religion, or level of experience.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, and sexual imagery or language.
- Deliberate intimidation, stalking, or following someone.
- Photographing or filming someone who has asked you not to.
- Sustained disruption of talks, runs, or judging.
- Threats or violence of any kind.

## Competing honestly

The following is expected of every team:

- Your robot must be your team's own work. Buying a finished robot, or entering
  someone else's, is not competing.
- Do not tamper with another team's robot, tools, or workspace. Do not interfere
  with a run in progress.
- Do not misrepresent your team's members, their student status, or their IEEE
  membership.
- Follow the [rulebook](/rules). Trying to find an advantage in an ambiguity is
  fine and often clever; concealing a breach is not.

## Safety in the venue

Robots involve batteries, motors and soldering irons. Follow the venue's safety
rules, keep your workspace clear, and do not leave charging batteries
unattended. Tell an organizer immediately if something is smoking, sparking, or
too hot to touch.

## Reporting a problem

If something happens to you, or you see it happen to someone else, tell an
organizer. On the day, any committee member or volunteer will do — they are
identifiable at the venue. Otherwise, reply to any email you have been sent, or
use the [FAQ page](/faq).

Reports are handled discreetly. Your name is not shared with the person you are
reporting unless you have been asked first.

If you are in immediate danger, contact venue security or the emergency services
first.

## What happens then

Every report is reviewed by the organizing committee. Depending on what is
found, the response may be a warning, a request to stop and leave the area, the
disqualification of a team, removal from the venue, or a bar from future MMRC
events. Where a team is disqualified for a conduct breach, the registration fee
is not refunded.

The person who reported the incident is told what was decided.

## Where this applies

The competition venue, any associated workshop or social event, the mailing
lists, and this website — including the game leaderboard, where a name is still
something other people read.
